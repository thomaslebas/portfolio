# Yeah Nah Maybe 2026

Subline: A voter advice app, revisited

Before I worked in product design I helped design two voter advice applications, VoteLocal in 2016 and On the Fence in 2017. Both were matching tools to help young New Zealanders start their political journey, and had a simple mechanism: answer some questions, get matched with candidates or political parties. However, they were also expensive and slow to build, and static by nature—the questions could only go so far.

With the advent of AI, vibe coding, and an election year rolling around in New Zealand, I decided to pick that thread back up. The same architecture, which once took a team, significant budget, and a lot of time, came together in a matter of days. Writing good questions and accurately representing where parties actually stand however—that's still tedious work. 

This time around, there was something new for me to explore: the interaction. Chat interfaces are normalised, common. AI models you can ask anything about, too. I decided to try this out with Yeah Nah Maybe 2026, where the user can keep going past the initial match, into the kind of nuance a fixed question set can't reach by asking follow up questions to Tua, an AI personified as the native tuatara whose been around long enough to know a lot about politics in Aotearoa.

As of August 2026, this project is still on the shelf.
