# Human slop

Subline: Off the screen and into the workshop
Meta: Linocut printing · Getting messy  · Touching grass
Thumbnail: humanslop-7.jpg

I've reflected a lot on the fact that my working life happens on a screen 99% of the time, and I realised I’ve been missing my art and design roots where I got my hands dirty. The age of AI is distancing me from that more and more, so I decided to try looking in the opposite direction: linocut printing. 

Linocut has always appealed. It suits my style—block colour, flat, graphic. It also gives me somewhere to apply my systems thinking outside of the digital: how to produce things repeatedly, with little waste, in different configurations. It's modularity, but physical.

Strangely enough it's also taught me something about my daily practice at work. Digital product design is usually a step removed from the final thing, where we produce instructions that engineers build from. But linocut isn't that. I'm working directly with the material, and the result is immediate. It made me understand what I’ve been experiencing and enjoying with AI coding tools: they let me work in the material of code directly rather than just specifying what someone else should build. 

Linocut hasn't just balanced my screen-heavy work, it's also helped understand what I’m wanting from it.
