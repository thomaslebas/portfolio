# Human slop

Subline: Off the screen and into the workshop

I've reflected a lot on the fact that my working life happens 99% on a screen, and I've been missing my art and design roots where I got my hands dirty. The age of AI is taking that making away from us more and more, so I decided to try looking in the other direction: linocut printing. 

Linocut has always appealed. It suits my style; block colour, flat, graphic. It also gives me somewhere to apply systems thinking outside of the digital: how to produce things repeatedly, with little waste, in different configurations. It's modularity, but physical.

Strangely enough it's also taught me something about my own practice. Digital product design is usually a step removed from the final thing; we produce instructions that engineers build from—but linocut isn't that. I'm working directly with the material, and the result is immediate. But that's also what I've been experiencing with AI coding tools: they let me work in the material of code directly rather than just specifying what someone else should build. 

Linocut hasn't just balanced the screen-heavy work, it's also helped explain what I was actually looking for from it.
